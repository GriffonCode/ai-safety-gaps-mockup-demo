Methodology & Limitations

Purpose

This analysis attempts to map structural gaps in the AI safety ecosystem: where organizations are concentrated, which research-to-impact pathways exist, and what commercial infrastructure is missing compared to mature fields like cybersecurity.

The goal is to surface patterns that might inform funding and strategy decisions. This is a sketch, not a census. We've prioritized breadth over depth, and transparency about uncertainty over false precision. Where we don't know something, we say so.

Limits

Our view of the field is shaped by what's publicly visible and what our sources happen to cover. Organizations outside EA-adjacent networks are likely underrepresented. Topic categorizations reflect one analyst's judgment and haven't been validated by practitioners. Pathways between research and decisions are inferred from public relationships, not interviews.

This is a January 2026 snapshot. The field moves fast.

Future Work

Improvements that would make this analysis more useful, organized by what they'd accomplish:

Increase accuracy in gap areas

The apparent gaps (incident response, liability, post-deployment monitoring) may reflect our limited visibility rather than true absence. Priority work:

  • Systematic search for organizations in apparent gap areas, including outside EA-adjacent networks
  • Verify whether "zero coverage" areas have emerging or informal work we've missed

Quantify resource allocation

Organization counts don't capture capacity. A 200-person lab and a 3-person nonprofit both count as "1 org."

  • Employee counts per organization (from team pages)
  • Funding per topic area (from grant data already collected)
  • Track how resources flow to apparent gap areas vs. crowded areas

Validate key pathways

The pathway analysis is inference. Actual influence flows require talking to people.

  • Interviews with practitioners on 3-5 crucial pathways (e.g., evaluations to deployment decisions)
  • Identify which pathways have highest expected impact if strengthened

Estimate impact magnitude

Not all gaps matter equally. Some areas may be small by design; others may represent large missed opportunities.

  • Expert estimates on importance of gap areas
  • Assessment of which pathways, if working, would most reduce risk
  • Comparison of resource allocation to rough importance rankings
