Methodology & Limitations

This is a working map of the AI safety ecosystem. We're sharing what we can see so others can help fill in blind spots.

What This Analysis Is

  • A proof of concept for ecosystem analysis methodology
  • A demonstration of how gaps could be identified systematically
  • Transparent about data limitations and sourcing
  • An invitation to scrutiny, not a claim of authority

What This Analysis Is Not

  • A comprehensive, fully-verified dataset
  • A production-ready tool with real-time updates
  • The final word on anything

Sourcing Standards

Every data point in this analysis falls into one of three levels:

Verified
Direct from source, cross-referenced
Data comes directly from organization websites or official databases, checked against 2+ sources where possible.
Inferred
Single source or reasonable inference
Marked with ~. Based on available evidence but not independently verified. Noted as estimates.
Illustrative
Placeholder for demonstration
Marked with *. Shows methodology structure but should not be cited as fact.

Key Limitations

Selection Bias

Our sources may over-represent certain types of organizations. Funders we track may not represent all AI safety funding. We're likely missing orgs that don't appear in EA-adjacent spaces.

Topic Categorization

Mapping organizations to topics is judgment-based. Different analysts might categorize differently. We haven't validated these mappings with practitioners.

Pathway Inference

We infer how research reaches decisions without interviewing the people involved. Actual influence pathways may differ significantly from what public sources suggest.

Point-in-Time Snapshot

This data reflects January 2026. The field changes rapidly. Orgs launch, pivot, or shut down. Grant priorities shift.

Future Work

To make this analysis more useful, we'd need:

Moderate effort

  • Track where SPAR/MATS alumni end up (pipeline flow)
  • Cross-reference with 80,000 Hours job board
  • Add employee counts from team pages (~60 pages, 2-3 hours)

Significant effort

  • Verify org-topic mappings with practitioners
  • Track funding amounts over time
  • Historical SPAR/MATS cohorts (SP25, SP24)

Would change the picture

  • Interview 20-30 practitioners on actual pathways (~30 hours)
  • Survey enterprise buyers on AI safety needs
  • Map informal networks and advisory relationships

If you can help with any of these, or know someone who can, we'd welcome the collaboration.
