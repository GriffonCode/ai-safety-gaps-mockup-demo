Gap 3: The Missing Commercial Layer

Cybersecurity has a mature ecosystem of insurers, auditors, and compliance tools. AI safety has almost none of this infrastructure.

Ecosystem Comparison

Every component that makes cybersecurity work in practice is absent or nascent for AI safety:

Component Cybersecurity AI Safety
Insurance market $10B+ market None
Third-party audits SOC2, ISO 27001 None
Professional certifications CISSP, CISM, CEH None
Compliance software Vanta, Drata, Secureframe None
Industry standards NIST CSF, CIS Controls NIST AI RMF (emerging)
Liability case law Established precedents None
C-suite role CISO standard Rare (no CAISO)
Vendor requirements In every enterprise RFP Rare, ad-hoc
Consulting practices All Big 4 + specialists Minimal

Missing Stakeholders

Who needs to enter the AI safety ecosystem for it to function commercially?

Present Today

  • Academic researchers (Oxford, Berkeley)
  • Policy think tanks (GovAI, CSET)
  • Nonprofit advocacy (FLI, CAIS)
  • Government bodies (UK/US AISIs)

Absent (Needed)

  • Insurance underwriters (Lloyd's, AIG, Munich Re)
  • Audit firms (Big 4 AI safety practices)
  • Compliance SaaS startups
  • Industry associations (like ISACA for cyber)
  • Certification bodies
  • Management consultants

Why This Matters

Without commercial incentives, AI safety remains voluntary. Insurance creates financial pressure. Audits create accountability. Certifications create career paths. This infrastructure took cybersecurity 20+ years to build. AI safety may not have that long.

The Commercial Incentive Loop

How cybersecurity became a business requirement (and how AI safety could):

Incidents occur
Breaches cause losses
Insurance emerges
Risk transfer market
Insurers require standards
Pricing demands data
Compliance industry
Tools, audits, certs

For AI: We're waiting at step 1. Major incidents haven't yet created the liability pressure that drives the rest.
